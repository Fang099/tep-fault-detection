Findings from validation

    Limitations

    • Short memory. Most models see the 52 variables at a single moment; the history versions add only the last 30 minutes as rolling means and standard deviations. Faults that drift over hours, or change the plant's dynamics in subtler ways, remain hard to see.
    • Some faults are invisible to these variables. Faults 3, 9 and 15 are widely reported as nearly undetectable in the TEP literature, because they barely change the statistics of the measured data. Low scores on them are expected.
    • Detection delays can include chance alarms. At a 5% false-alarm rate, three alarms in a row eventually happen on normal operation too, so long delays for weakly detected faults overstate what the model can do.
    • Thresholds are chosen on validation. On new data the actual false-alarm rate can drift from the 5% target. The final test therefore also reports the exact validated model (trained on the training runs only), whose threshold fits it exactly.
    • History windows warm up. At the start of every run a rolling window holds fewer samples than its length. In training and validation the fault starts after 1 hour, so windows longer than that are never full when a fault begins; they are shown but not used to choose the best model.
    • Training subsamples time steps. To keep training time practical, models train on every few time steps of each run; validation and test use every row.
    • Simulation only. Conclusions are limited to the TEP benchmark and may not carry over to a real plant.
