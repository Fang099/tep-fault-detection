Task 1 · Fault detection

Is a fault happening right now?

Each model reads one 3-minute snapshot of the 52 process variables and outputs a fault score. An alarm goes off when the score passes a threshold. Pick a model, move the threshold, and see what it catches and what it misses.

1The alarm threshold

Rows above the threshold raise an alarm. Lowering it catches more faults but also raises more false alarms on normal operation. Every model on this site is compared at the threshold that gives 5% false alarms on validation data.

2Why errors happen: overlapping scores

How the model's scores are distributed for normal rows and for faulty rows. Wherever the two overlap, no threshold can separate them: anything left of the line is called normal, anything right of it raises an alarm. Click a fault in section 4 to add its rows to this chart.

3Every threshold at once: the ROC curve

Each point on the curve is one threshold: the share of faulty rows caught against the share of normal rows that raise a false alarm. A useless model follows the diagonal. The dot is your current threshold.

PR-AUC summarizes precision against recall over all thresholds. Because 91% of validation rows are faulty, even the majority baseline scores about 0.91 on it, so ROC-AUC and the false alarm rate are more informative here.

4Fault by fault

Share of each fault's rows caught at the current threshold. Some faults barely change the measurements and are nearly impossible to see in a single snapshot. Click a fault for details.

5How quickly is a fault detected?

For each faulty validation run: minutes from the moment the fault starts until the model raises 3 alarms in a row. Bars show the median over runs; faded bars mean the fault was detected in only some runs. These were computed in advance at fixed false alarm rates, so they don't follow the threshold slider.

False alarm rate

Read long delays with care. At a 5% false alarm rate, a normal process also raises occasional alarms, and over many hours three in a row eventually happen by chance. Faults that are only "detected" after many hours, in some runs, are likely such chance alarms rather than real detections. A reference measured on fault-free runs will be added to separate the two.
