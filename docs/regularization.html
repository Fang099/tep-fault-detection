What does regularization do here?

Ridge (L2) shrinks every coefficient toward zero; lasso (L1) pushes some of them to exactly zero, switching sensors off. The strength C runs from 1e-6 (very strong) to 1 (weak). Watch performance and the coefficients change as C moves.

Training picks the weights w that minimize a penalty on their size plus C times the total log-loss over the N training rows (this is how scikit-learn writes it):

Dividing everything by C·N doesn't change which weights win, and shows the trade-off more plainly: the average log-loss per row, plus the penalty scaled by 1 / (C·N).

The difference is easiest to see for a single coefficient. Say that without a penalty its best value would be β^. In the simplest case (squared error, inputs uncorrelated), adding a penalty of strength λ gives:

Ridge divides, so a coefficient gets smaller but never reaches zero. Lasso subtracts a fixed amount, so any coefficient smaller than λ lands exactly on zero and that sensor drops out of the model.
